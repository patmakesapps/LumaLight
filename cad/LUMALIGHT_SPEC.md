# LumaLight — enclosure spec (v2.1, open back, 2026-10-12)

Modelled in `LumaLight.blend` (Blender 5.1, 1 BU = 1 mm). Assembly sits at the origin in **LUMALIGHT ASSEMBLY**
(`LUMALIGHT PRINT PARTS` + linked electronics in `LUMALIGHT PLACED PARTS`). Electronics are parked at x 300+ in
numbered collections; the L-mark and wordmark are in `LOGOS`. Body coordinates: X = width (centred), Y = depth
(front outer face y = 0, back of the panel y = 56), Z up from the counter. Cutaway views are in `views/`.

The print parts are generated by `build_lumalight.py` (CadQuery 2.8 on Python 3.12). It writes the print-oriented
STLs to `STL/` and assembly-oriented `asm_*.stl` meshes (to `%LUMALIGHT_ASM_DIR%`, default a temp folder) that are
imported into the blend to replace the part meshes. Change the numbers in the script, not the meshes.

## Why v2

v1 (bottom plate + spine, 40 deep) failed on the bench: the dupont housings on the ESP32 pins pointed at the
front wall with 0.5 mm to the ring pocket, and the ToF board had to be threaded down from the top past them with
its JST and cable attached (`bench reference/bad_design _01/_02.jpg`). v2 opens the **back** instead of the bottom,
hangs the ESP32 on the back panel, gives the wires an 18 mm bay, and makes the sensor a push-in-from-behind part.
Everything can be wired on the bench first.

## v2.1 (same day, after the sensor coupon)

The v2 pocket coupon showed the slit sitting about 3 mm below the real VL53L1X package, and the two snap fingers
barely holding the board. The board blockout had been eyeballed from photos (26.5 tall, package 21.5 up). The
CQRobot board is really 23 x 28.5 with R4 corners, both Ø3 holes at the top corners, the package 24.65 above the
cable edge. The ROLX-4 model of the board (`ROLX-4/cad/ROLX-4.blend`) is now appended and used in the assembly.
The sensor is screwed to two blocks instead of clipped, the window is re-centred on the package, and the ESP32
standoffs on the panel get a relieved neck (see the ESP32 row; the v2 panel already printed stays in use).

## Concept

An upright white monolith, 62 × 56 × 100 mm (body 53 deep + 3 mm back panel), vertical front edges chamfered 2.5,
top edges 1 mm. The front carries a Ø46 halo (transparent diffuser disc, NeoPixel ring behind it) and an 11 × 7 mm
rounded "visor" port for the ToF sensor below it. The LUMALIEN L-mark is recessed into the top face with purple
glued inlays; **LUMALIGHT** is recessed on the front near the base, **LUMALIEN** on the back panel. The back panel is
held by 4 screws into corner bosses; the USB-C cable leaves through a notch at the bottom of the panel.

## Print parts (5)

| Part | Material | Orientation |
|---|---|---|
| LUMALIGHT BODY | white | **front face on the bed**, open back up, no supports. The front gets the plate texture; every inner feature (rib, tabs, seats, blocks, bosses) grows straight up |
| LUMALIGHT BACK PANEL | white | outer face on the bed, standoffs and lip up, no supports |
| LUMALIGHT DIFFUSER | transparent | flange face down (bed texture frosts it) |
| LUMALIGHT LOGO INLAY L / TRI | purple | flat |
| TEST_SENSOR_POCKET (`STL/test/`) | any | front face down. 44 × 48 slab of the front wall with the pocket, window, screw blocks and floor stub, ~20 min. Print this before the body |

STLs in `STL/`, print orientation, 1:1 mm. Hardware: 4 × M2.5 × 6 self-tap (ESP32 top pair, sensor) and 6 × M2.5 × 8
self-tap (ESP32 USB-end pair, panel to body). No inserts, no magnets, every screw driven along its axis from
outside the part. Glue: diffuser, inlays.

## Assembly order (everything pre-wired)

1. On the bench: screw the ESP32 to the panel standoffs, components toward the panel, pins toward you. Feed the
   USB-C cable through the panel notch from the outside and plug it in. Plug the sensor's leads and the ring's
   leads onto the pins. The whole harness is now live and the body is still empty.
2. Sensor: hold the board by its edges with the JST toward the front wall and the cable hanging down. Push it
   straight into the pocket from the back between the cheeks until its face sits on the seats. Line the two top
   holes up on the pilots (you can see them through the holes) and run in 2 × M2.5 × 6. Tuck the cable under
   the board toward the back.
3. Ring: in from the front through the halo hole, wires first, LEDs facing out, until it seats on the three tabs.
   Turn it so the solder pads sit between tabs. The panel just lies behind the body while you do this; the leads
   are long enough to stay plugged in.
4. Glue the diffuser into the front recess. The ring is now captured.
5. Bring the panel up to the body, fold the dupont leads into the bay, seat the lip, 4 screws from the back.
6. Glue the purple inlays into the top recess.

To service it: 4 screws, the panel comes away with the ESP32 on it and everything stays plugged in.

## Verify on the bench before/after printing

- Sensor screw spacing: the pilots are 16.4 mm apart, between the vendor drawing (16.1) and the ROLX-4 model
  (16.8); the board's Ø3 holes give ±0.25 mm on an M2.5. Caliper the real centre spacing; outside 15.9..16.9 the
  pilots move.
- Window: with the board screwed into the coupon, both package apertures must be fully visible through the
  window with clear margin all round. The window is centred 24.65 mm above the cable edge (drawing and ROLX-4
  model agree); if the real package sits elsewhere the window moves, nothing else does.
- The JST is modelled 4.6 tall (ROLX-4 model) with 2.4 mm to the wall; the earlier 6 mm guess also cleared.
- Dupont housings modelled 14 mm long on 2.5 mm header plastic. Bay is 18.4 mm; the ring pads sit at y 8.3.
- Cable under the sensor: 9.3 mm from the cable edge to the floor (was 12 in v2; the board moved down with the
  real package height). Check the six silicone leads turn back without pushing on the board.

## Suggested print order

1. `STL/test/TEST_SENSOR_POCKET.stl` (~20 min): push the real board in, run the two screws, look through the
   window, check the cable turns under the board.
2. `LUMALIGHT_BACK_PANEL.stl` (v2.1 standoffs; the printed v2 panel is fine for this build): screw the ESP32 on
   with all leads plugged, hold a ruler against it. From the panel's inner face the housing tips reach 25 mm; the
   ring's back is 44.7 mm away, the front wall 50 mm.
3. Only then the body.
